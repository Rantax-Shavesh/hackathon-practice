Electrons & Voltage

Electric current isn't a flow of energy — it's a drift of charge. Adjust voltage and resistance to see how the electric field pushes electrons through a conductor.

Physics refresher

Ohm's Law: I = V / R — Current equals voltage divided by resistance.

Power: P = V × I = V² / R — Energy dissipated per second as heat.

Electron drift: In copper at 1 A, electrons drift ~0.1 mm/s — but the field propagates near light speed.

How the adder works

A full adder adds three bits (A, B, Carry-in) producing Sum and Carry-out:

Sum = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin
Cout = (A ∧ B) ∨ (A ∧ Cin) ∨ (B ∧ Cin)

Four full adders chained form a ripple-carry adder — the carry "ripples" from LSB to MSB. Modern CPUs use faster carry-lookahead adders.

Physics of scaling

Dynamic Power: P = C × V² × f — Power scales linearly with frequency and quadratically with voltage.

Thermal Throttling: When temperature exceeds Tjunction (typically 100°C), the CPU reduces frequency/voltage to prevent damage.

Dennard Scaling Ended: ~2006 — voltage could no longer scale with frequency, ending "free" performance gains.

How backpropagation works

Forward Pass: Input → Hidden layers (ReLU) → Output (Sigmoid) → Binary Cross-Entropy Loss.

Backward Pass: Chain rule computes gradients ∂L/∂w for each weight. Weights update: w ← w − η × ∂L/∂w.

Decision Boundary: The network learns a non-linear boundary. More hidden units = more complex boundaries (but risk overfitting).
